    Catalogue notation¶

    An example specifies a smooth projective variety X X , its geometric construction, and an ordered basis for the computed ambient cohomology. This page fixes the notation used for its matrix, grading, spectral factors and atomic invariants.

    Chern index¶

    For a positive-dimensional Fano variety, the intrinsic index is the largest positive integer I I for which −KX=IH − K X = I H with H∈Pic(X) H ∈ Pic ( X ) . The catalogue's Recorded Chern index retains the convention of the given presentation; for the original flag computations it is the gcd of the ambient first-Chern coefficients. A smooth conic has ambient gcd one in P2 P 2 and intrinsic index two. For a zero-dimensional variety this gcd is not a geometric Fano index.

    Quantum parameters and spectral factors¶

    The tuple y=(yi)i∈I y = ( y i ) i ∈ I consists of the reported Novikov coordinates, in their stated order. The label set I I need not be consecutive. If ηi η i is the divisor class defining coordinate yi y i , then the formal curve monomial is

    qβ=∏i∈Iyiηi⋅β. q β = ∏ i ∈ I y i η i ⋅ β .

    The software names these coordinates y1, y2, and so on, retaining ambient node labels in the type-A convention. The accompanying map identifies the variables with the kept roots or chosen Novikov divisors; variable names alone do not identify curve coordinates.

    An analysis point a a is the stated assignment of values to these coordinates, at which all matrix entries are defined. Write A(a)=A(y)|y=a A ( a ) = A ( y ) | y = a . A label A(1) A ( 1 ) means that every Novikov coordinate is set to one. The characteristic polynomial and unit cyclic determinant are

In a factorization into monic, pairwise coprime, square-free factors, if pj(z) p j ( z ) occurs to power mj m j , each of its distinct roots is an eigenvalue of algebraic multiplicity mj m j .

    Full cohomology and atomic invariants¶

    The ambient dimension N N , the full Hodge-invariant dimension and the total cohomology dimension are different quantities. The rational Hodge-invariant subspace is

    HHod∗(X)=⨁p(H2p(X,Q)∩Hp,p(X)). H Hod ∗ ( X ) = ⨁ p ( H 2 p ( X , Q ) ∩ H p , p ( X ) ) .

    Its complexification is the invariant space for the Hodge group. Ambient classes lie in this subspace, but additional rational Hodge classes may occur in primitive cohomology. The full Hodge numbers are hp,q(X)=dimC⁡Hq(X,ΩXp) h p , q ( X ) = dim C ⁡ H q ( X , Ω X p ) , and the total cohomology dimension is ∑p,qhp,q(X) ∑ p , q h p , q ( X ) . In particular, neither the sum of diagonal Hodge numbers nor the matrix size automatically equals the rational Hodge-invariant dimension.

    A coarse Hodge atom is the rational Hodge-group representation attached to a generic local spectral component of the quantum connection. For an atom represented by Eα E α , write ECα=Eα⊗QC E C α = E α ⊗ Q C . The Hodge group Hod Hod is the kernel of the Mumford–Tate group's character on the Tate line Q(1) Q ( 1 ) . The atomic invariants are

    ρα=dimC⁡(ECα)HodC,Pα(t)=∑k∈ZdimC⁡(ECα)ktk, ρ α = dim C ⁡ ( E C α ) Hod C , P α ( t ) = ∑ k ∈ Z dim C ⁡ ( E C α ) k t k ,

    where k k is the weight of the Hodge cocharacter. A class of Hodge type (p,q) ( p , q ) has weight p−q p − q . Thus the full Hodge polynomial is

    PX(t)=∑p,qhp,q(X)tp−q. P X ( t ) = ∑ p , q h p , q ( X ) t p − q .

    All coefficients are nonnegative dimensions; no alternating signs are inserted for odd cohomology. The value Pα(1) P α ( 1 ) is the full rank of the atom. The constant coefficient of Pα P α counts cocharacter-weight-zero directions and can exceed ρα ρ α .

    The unit-cyclic criterion identifies full coarse factors only after the computed space is identified with the full Hodge-invariant sector and unit cyclicity is established at an admissible point with the required geometric persistence. Determining their full Hodge polynomials also requires the action on the remaining cohomology. In a full coarse decomposition, sum one Pα P α for each atom, with the indicated multiplicity, to obtain PX P X . A rational characteristic factor alone does not determine the Hodge polynomial of each conjugate root. See atomic invariants.

    Export compatibility¶

    Some connection-analysis exports use the negative of the matrix displayed here. If such an export has a monic characteristic factor p(z) p ( z ) of degree r r , the corresponding monic factor of A A is (−1)rp(−z) ( − 1 ) r p ( − z ) . If B=−A B = − A uses the same basis and unit, its cyclic determinant satisfies ΔA=(−1)N(N−1)/2ΔB Δ A = ( − 1 ) N ( N − 1 ) / 2 Δ B . The grading matrix and the underlying connection are unchanged. All catalogue eigenvalue labels and spectral conditions refer to A(a) A ( a ) .

    In exact-data exports, null in an exponent field supplies no exponent value. It must not be interpreted as an empty multiset or as the zero exponent.

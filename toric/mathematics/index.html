    Toric methods and conventions¶

    A fan describes the ambient smooth projective toric variety and its divisor and curve lattices. A sum of globally generated line bundles defines the complete intersection whose genus-zero invariants are computed by Euler-twisted localization.

    Fan and divisor inputs¶

    Let \(\Sigma\) be a complete smooth projective fan in a lattice \(N\), with primitive rays \(v_\rho\), and let \(X_\Sigma\) have dimension \(n\). The software preserves the ray order and enters each maximal cone by zero-based ray indices.

    Write \(D_\rho\) for the invariant divisors. The rational Chow ring is

    \[ A^*(X_\Sigma)_{\mathbb Q} =\mathbb Q[D_\rho:\rho\in\Sigma(1)]/(I_\Sigma+J_\Sigma), \]

    where \(I_\Sigma\) is the Stanley–Reisner ideal and \(J_\Sigma\) is generated by \(\sum_\rho\langle m,v_\rho\rangle D_\rho\), for \(m\in\operatorname{Hom}(N,\mathbb Z)\).

    A full row of K describes a divisor \(D_\ell=\sum_\rho k_{\ell\rho}D_\rho\), with one coefficient per ray. Short rows instead use the reported cohomology divisor basis. That basis must not be confused with the chosen Novikov divisors. Negative ray coefficients can represent a nef divisor: the relevant test is degree on invariant curves.

    Complete intersections and pushed curve classes¶

    Let \(E=\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^{s}L_\ell\), with globally generated line bundles \(L_\ell\), and let a regular section have smooth zero locus \(Y\subset X_\Sigma\), with inclusion \(\iota\). Then

    \[ \dim Y=n-s,\qquad c_1(TY)=\iota^*\left(\sum_\rho D_\rho-\sum_{\ell=1}^{s}D_\ell\right). \]

    The interface checks nontrivial nef defining summands; it does not certify smoothness of a chosen section.

    For \(\gamma_k=\iota^*\alpha_k\), with \(\alpha_k\in H^{2q_k}(X_\Sigma)\), Theorem 3.17 assumes

    \[ \sum_{k=1}^{m}q_k=n-s+m-3+ \int_\beta\bigl(c_1(TX_\Sigma)-c_1(E)\bigr). \]

    Its left-hand side is the sum of invariants

    \[ \sum_{\substack{\beta'\in H_2(Y,\mathbb Z)\\\iota_*\beta'=\beta}} \langle\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m\rangle^Y_{0,\beta'}. \]

    Thus the ambient curve coordinate alone does not distinguish different target classes with the same pushforward. The ample complete-intersection and dimension hypotheses discussed in Appendix B supply the relevant homology identifications in the supported cases; nefness alone should not be substituted for all of those hypotheses.

    Decorated-tree localization¶

    For nonzero effective \(\beta\), Theorem 3.12 indexes the fixed loci by decorated trees \(\Gamma=(G,\ell,d,\mathrm{mk})\). Vertices are labelled by maximal cones, edges by invariant curves and covering degrees \(d_e>0\), and \(\mathrm{mk}\) assigns the marked points. In the reduced ambient basis,

    \[ \langle\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m\rangle^{X_\Sigma}_{0,\beta} =\sum_\Gamma \frac{\displaystyle \prod_{v\in V}e_{\ell(v)}^{\operatorname{val}(v)-1} \prod_{e\in E}\Omega_e\prod_{v\in V}I_v \prod_{k=1}^{m}s_{\ell(\mathrm{mk}(k))}(\gamma_k) }{|\operatorname{Aut}\Gamma|\displaystyle\prod_{e\in E}d_e}. \]

    Here \(s_\sigma\) is fixed-point restriction, \(e_\sigma\) is the tangent Euler class, and \(\Omega_e\) is the edge factor defined in §3.3. With \(n_v=\operatorname{val}(v)+|\mathrm{mk}^{-1}(v)|\),

    \[ I_v=\left(\prod_{e\ni v}\omega_{v,e}^{-1}\right) \left(\sum_{e\ni v}\omega_{v,e}^{-1}\right)^{n_v-3} \quad(n_v\ge3). \]

    For unstable vertices the paper uses \(\Theta_v\): one edge/no markings gives \(\omega_{v,e}\); one edge/one marking gives \(1\); two edges/no markings give \((\omega_{v,e_1}+\omega_{v,e_2})^{-1}\).

    For the complete intersection, Theorem 3.17 inserts \(\operatorname{Tw}(\Gamma;\lambda_{\mathrm{aux}})\) into this summand, uses the ambient lifts \(\alpha_k\), and specializes the auxiliary Euler parameters and positive-degree torus classes after taking the complete sum. Torus evaluations in the software are exact; independent evaluations are a consistency check when enabled. New saved fivefold-queue records explicitly used one evaluation per class, while older caches may have two.

    No insertion is required for the cubic-surface line count: projective_space(3).gw([], beta=(1,1,1,1), K=[[3]]) returns 27. The unstable-vertex rules do not create extra marked points. The toric degree-zero GW entry path uses its documented three-insertion classical convention.

    Curve coordinates and Novikov monomials¶

    In the ray-relation presentation, \(\beta=(b_\rho)\) satisfies \(\sum_\rho b_\rho v_\rho=0\). Coordinates in the reported integral h2_basis are also accepted. An effective curve relation can have negative entries.

    Choose divisor classes \(A_1,\ldots,A_r\) for the quantum coordinates and writes

    \[ q^\beta=\prod_{\nu=1}^{r}y_\nu^{A_\nu\cdot\beta}. \]

    These \(A_\nu\) are the reported novikov_divisors; y1,y2,... follow their order, not ray numbers. A fallback divisor basis can give Laurent monomials. Keep this map with every result.

    The toric-ambient operator¶

    Let \(H^*_{X_\Sigma}(Y)=\operatorname{im}(H^*(X_\Sigma,\mathbb Q)\to H^*(Y,\mathbb Q))\), with nondegenerate restricted pairing and basis \(\{\sigma_i\}\). The ambient operator is

    \[ A(q)\sigma_i=\sum_{\beta\in\mathrm{NE}(X_\Sigma)}\sum_{j,k} \left(\sum_{\iota_*\beta'=\beta} \langle c_1(TY),\sigma_i,\sigma_j\rangle^Y_{0,\beta'}\right) q^\beta g^{jk}\sigma_k, \qquad g_{jk}=\int_Y\sigma_j\smile\sigma_k. \]

    This is the ambient projection of small quantum multiplication. The software returns divisor-polynomial basis names, the matrix and its grading. Reducing the ambient pairing does not add primitive cohomology or establish equality with the full Hodge-invariant module.

    Finite curve searches¶

    The dimension condition gives the bound \((c_1(TX_\Sigma)-c_1(E))\cdot\beta\le\dim Y+1\) for matrix contributions. If the residual divisor is ample on the ambient Mori cone, this yields a finite ambient search. If \(Y\) is Fano but the residual is not ample on \(X_\Sigma\), the additional positivity hypothesis of Proposition B.3 is relevant; target Fano positivity alone is not an ambient stopping rule.

    Keep the returned curve list and enumeration mode. Supported modes distinguish positive ambient bounds, conditional Mori searches, suggested inequalities and ample-degree cutoffs. A sampled inequality check is not a proof of the Mori cone; a cutoff is exact only on its selected region.

    Quantum multiplication, grading and the connection¶

    Choose a homogeneous basis \(\{T_i\}\), the Poincaré pairing \(g_{ab}=\int_Z T_a\smile T_b\), and its inverse \((g^{ab})\). In this notation,

    \[ T_i\star_{\mathrm{small}}T_j =\sum_{a,b}\left(\sum_{\beta\in\mathrm{NE}(Z)} \langle T_i,T_j,T_a\rangle^Z_{0,\beta}\,q^\beta\right)g^{ab}T_b. \]

    Here \(Z\) denotes the target of this full-cohomology formula. The zero-class term is cup product. A matrix requires the inverse pairing, not merely a table of three-point invariants. A three-point genus-zero bracket has three insertions; subscripts indicating the number of markings and the genus are distinguished by context.

    The matrix returned by the software is denoted \(A(q)\). It represents the ambient projection of multiplication by the first Chern class. Columns are images of basis vectors. In a homogeneous basis the grading matrix is \(G=[\mathrm{Gr}]\), with

    \[ \mathrm{Gr}|_{H^a(Z)}=\frac{a-\dim Z}{2}\,\mathrm{id},\qquad G_{ii}=\operatorname{codim}(T_i)-\frac{\dim Z}{2}. \]

    When the computed sector is identified with the Hodge-invariant module under the required geometric hypotheses, the small-locus connection has the convention

    \[ \nabla_{\partial_u}=\partial_u-\frac{A(q)}{u^2}+\frac{G}{u}. \]

    The saved analysis code also writes \(K^F=-A(q)\), so the same connection is \(\partial_u+K^F/u^2+G/u\). A characteristic factor \(p(z)\) of \(K^F\) corresponds to eigenvalues \(\lambda\) of \(A\) satisfying \(p(-\lambda)=0\). This sign change must accompany the eigenvalue labels; the grading is unchanged.

    Regularized-exponent multisets¶

    Write a supported formal factor as

    \[ M_{b,\alpha}\simeq\mathcal E^{\lambda(b)/u}\otimes R_{b,\lambda}. \]

    Here \(\lambda(b)\) is an eigenvalue of \(\kappa_b=\mathrm{Eu}\star_b(-)\); on the small locus the Euler class is \(c_1(TZ)\). If \(R_{b,\lambda}\) is regular singular with rational residue eigenvalues, its regularized-exponent multiset is

    \[ F_{b,\lambda} =\{\!\{-\theta+\mathbb Z:\theta\text{ is a residue eigenvalue of }R_{b,\lambda}\}\!\}. \]

    Every occurrence is retained with its algebraic multiplicity. Thus \(\{\!\{0,\tfrac12,\tfrac12\}\!\}\) has three entries; it must not be simplified to a two-element set. The software's admissible powers \(s=-\theta\) give these classes modulo \(\mathbb Z\). The powers and residue-class lists are multisets, not a termwise pairing of sorted lists.

    Catalogue summaries keep the specialization point and factor association. When one rational characteristic factor has several roots, the displayed multiset applies separately at each indicated eigenvalue. Partial and unresolved calculations remain labelled; a missing exponent is not zero.

    The unit cyclic determinant is

    \[ \Delta(b)=\det\bigl(1,A(b)1,\ldots,A(b)^{N-1}1\bigr), \]

    where \(N\) is the dimension of the space on which the matrix acts. Its nonvanishing proves that the unit is cyclic on that space. Identifying this with a generic Hodge factor additionally requires the ambient/Hodge comparison and persistence hypotheses. A numerical specialization used to display eigenvalues does not itself verify those hypotheses or the admissibility of a point in the theoretical parameter domain.

    Hodge data and the scope of rationality statements¶

    Fan cohomology gives the ambient Hodge diamond. Full complete-intersection Hodge calculations require the implemented positivity and dimension hypotheses; some nef fourfold records instead cite literature values. In fivefolds, primitive middle \(H^5\) is odd and lies outside the displayed ambient even-sector matrix.

    The cyclic-factor obstruction applies to dimensions 2–4 and requires a cyclic generic Hodge factor of unramified exponential type with rational residues. Its separate fivefold criterion, Theorem 4.10 attributed to Cavenaghi–Katzarkov–Kontsevich, assumes a coarse Hodge atom \(\alpha\) with \(\rho_\alpha\le3\) and \([t^3]P_\alpha(t)>0\). A small-matrix eigenvalue or a computed exponent list alone does not establish these hypotheses. The catalogue therefore preserves the computations without assigning automatic fivefold rationality verdicts.

    Sources and reproducibility¶

    References: the toric article, Theorems 3.12 and 3.17, Definition 4.2, §4.3 and Appendix B. Their localization framework follows the cited work of Liu, Liu–Sheshmani and Graber–Pandharipande. The flag paper supplies the parallel homogeneous calculation.

    The universal catalogue is organized by geometric dimension and construction. Its CKP and HLM identifiers refer to Coates–Kasprzyk–Prince, Four-dimensional Fano toric complete intersections, and Hausen–Laface–Mauz, On smooth Fano fourfolds of Picard number two. CKP period buckets do not identify varieties solely by equality of periods.

    The saved datasets retain the exact inputs, basis, grading and analysis point. Their validation is not a new localization run of every entry. The software guides distinguish Python, terminal and interface input styles.

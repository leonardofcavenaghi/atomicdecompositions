    Fanography and algebraic-geometry databases¶

    Modern classification theory in algebraic geometry — from smooth Fano threefolds and generalized Grassmannians to reflexive polytopes and four-dimensional Fano complete intersections — increasingly relies on structured, online classification databases. Rather than leaving explicit constructions, numerical invariants, birational maps, and derived-category data dispersed across decades of papers, projects such as Fanography, Grassmannian.info, the Graded Ring Database (GRDB), the Fanosearch quantum-period datasets, polyDB, and the Kreuzer–Skarke Calabi–Yau archive assemble them into searchable, cross-linked references.

    GEOATOMS sits alongside these databases as a specialized quantum-cohomological and Hodge-atomic companion. Where existing atlases index classical geometry, birational structure, polytopes, and one-parameter quantum periods, GEOATOMS provides exact symbolic small quantum multiplication matrices A(y)=c1(TX)⋆ A ( y ) = c 1 ( T X ) ⋆ , unit cyclic determinants ΔA(a) Δ A ( a ) , regularized-exponent multisets Fa,λ F a , λ of the quantum connection, and coarse Hodge-atom invariants (ρα,Pα(t)) ( ρ α , P α ( t ) ) , cross-referenced directly to Fanography, CKP, and HLM identifiers.

    What distinguishes a Fanography-type database¶

    In algebraic geometry, the informal label Fanography-type database refers to an interactive, invariant-indexed classification browser modeled on Fanography and its sister sites. Five structural features distinguish these resources from static tables in research articles or raw data archives:

    1. Canonical family identifiers. Every deformation family or combinatorial equivalence class carries a stable, human-readable label (for example, r-n in Fanography, Dynkin-type and node pairs in Grassmannian.info, or sequential database IDs in GRDB) that serves as a shared citation key across papers and software.
    2. Invariant-indexed geography. Varieties can be browsed, plotted, or filtered by discrete numerical invariants — such as anticanonical degree (−KX)3 ( − K X ) 3 , genus g g , Picard rank ρ ρ , intermediate-Jacobian dimension h1,2 h 1 , 2 , Chern numbers (c12,c2) ( c 1 2 , c 2 ) , Hilbert series, or period-sequence coefficients — exposing both populated regions and open gaps in geography.
    3. Explicit geometric realizations. Entries record concrete constructions (complete intersections, zero loci of homogeneous vector bundles, projective bundles, weighted hypersurfaces, or lattice polytopes and fans), often listing multiple equivalent presentations of the same deformation family.
    4. Inter-object relations. Objects are linked by geometric morphisms and equivalences: blow-ups and extremal contractions between Fano threefolds, Type I–IV projections and unprojections of Q Q -Fano varieties, mutations of mirror Laurent polynomials, and projective or homological projective dualities.
    5. Per-property provenance and machine-readable exports. Individual assertions carry direct literature citations, while underlying datasets are exposed as structured files, versioned archives (such as Zenodo DOIs), or programmatic APIs — an architecture exemplified more broadly in mathematics by the LMFDB.

    Families of algebraic-geometry databases¶

    Interactive geometric atlases: Fanography, Grassmannian.info, and sister sites¶

    Fanography is the canonical visual atlas of the 105 deformation families of smooth Fano threefolds established by Iskovskikh and Mori–Mukai. Families are indexed as r-n, where r=ρ(X)=b2(X)=h1,1(X)∈{1,…,10} r = ρ ( X ) = b 2 ( X ) = h 1 , 1 ( X ) ∈ { 1 , … , 10 } is the Picard rank (17 families with ρ=1 ρ = 1 , 36 with ρ=2 ρ = 2 , 31 with ρ=3 ρ = 3 , 13 with ρ=4 ρ = 4 , 3 with ρ=5 ρ = 5 , and one family P1×S10−ρ P 1 × S 10 − ρ for each ρ∈{6,7,8,9,10} ρ ∈ { 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 } ). Each family page aggregates classical numerical invariants — anticanonical degree (−KX)3 ( − K X ) 3 , genus g=h0(X,−KX)−2=12(−KX)3+1 g = h 0 ( X , − K X ) − 2 = 1 2 ( − K X ) 3 + 1 , Fano index, h1,2(X) h 1 , 2 ( X ) , full Hodge diamond, and polyvector parallelogram — alongside deformation data (dim⁡Aut0(X) dim ⁡ Aut 0 ( X ) , moduli counts), birational geometry (rationality, unirationality, stable rationality, primitivity, and the directed graph of blow-ups and extremal contractions), homogeneous zero-locus descriptions, semiorthogonal decompositions, K-stability status, coregularity, Futaki invariants, generic semisimplicity of quantum cohomology, and direct links to regularized quantum periods in GRDB.

    Grassmannian.info extends the same design to rational homogeneous varieties G/P G / P — a "periodic table of generalized Grassmannians" covering classical and exceptional Dynkin types, the Freudenthal magic square, the Vogel plane, and horospherical varieties. Alongside basic invariants (dimension, Fano index, Betti numbers, Aut0(G/P) Aut 0 ( G / P ) , minimal projective embeddings, and Hilbert series), individual entries document projective duality, full exceptional collections in Db(G/P) D b ( G / P ) , Lefschetz collections and homological projective duals, k k -Fanoness, and known results on small and big quantum cohomology semisimplicity together with eigenvalue multiplicities of c1(G/P)⋆ c 1 ( G / P ) ⋆ .

    Three closely related interactive browsers complete this cluster: hyperkaehler.info catalogs deformation invariants of the known compact hyperkähler families; le superficie algebriche plots the Enriques–Kodaira geography of minimal smooth complex surfaces in the (c12,c2) ( c 1 2 , c 2 ) -plane; and Mgnbar.info charts the Kodaira dimension, tautological Chow generation, and finite-field point-count polynomiality of M―g,n M ― g , n and Mg,n M g , n . Their computational companion, the Hodge diamond cutter, computes Hodge diamonds for flag varieties, complete intersections, and blow-ups.

    Graded rings and mirror-symmetry period databases: GRDB and Fanosearch¶

    The Graded Ring Database (GRDB), built by Gavin Brown and Alexander Kasprzyk with foundational input from Miles Reid and collaborators, organizes algebraic varieties through their polarized graded rings R(X,A)=⨁m≥0H0(X,mA) R ( X , A ) = ⨁ m ≥ 0 H 0 ( X , m A ) . Its search tables span polarized and elliptic K3 surfaces, Q Q -Fano 3-folds with terminal quotient singularities (indexed by Hilbert series, weights, singularity baskets, Kawamata and Bogomolov numbers, and Type I–IV projections and unprojections), terminal and canonical toric Fano 3-folds, smooth toric Fano varieties up to dimension 6 (from Øbro's algorithm, arXiv:0704.0049), Calabi–Yau 3-fold hypersurfaces and orbifolds, and quasismooth Fano and Calabi–Yau 4-fold weighted hypersurfaces.

    Closely integrated with GRDB is the Fanosearch programme ("Periodic Table of Shapes", Coates–Corti–Galkin–Golyshev–Kasprzyk–Prince et al.; arXiv:1212.1722, arXiv:1212.1785, arXiv:1303.3288), which approaches Fano classification via mirror symmetry. To a smooth Fano manifold X X one associates its regularized quantum period,

    G^X(t)=1+∑d≥2cdtd, G ^ X ( t ) = 1 + ∑ d ≥ 2 c d t d ,

    a power series encoding descendant one-point genus-zero Gromov–Witten invariants of anticanonical degree d d , together with an annihilating Picard–Fuchs differential operator L L and mirror Laurent polynomials (Minkowski polynomials up to mutation equivalence). The complete databases in dimensions 1, 2, and 3 and the expanding database in dimension 4 are published under a public-domain (CC0) license on Zenodo alongside Coates–Kasprzyk (arXiv:2112.00632):

    • Dimension 1: doi:10.5281/zenodo.5708188
    • Dimension 2 (del Pezzo surfaces): doi:10.5281/zenodo.5708232
    • Dimension 3 (all 105 smooth Fano threefolds): doi:10.5281/zenodo.5708272
    • Dimension 4 (known four-dimensional Fano manifolds, including CKP toric complete intersections): doi:10.5281/zenodo.5708307

    Interactive search interfaces for these period sequences are hosted directly on GRDB as 3d Minkowski period sequences and 4-dimensional quantum periods.

    Polytope, toric, and Calabi–Yau repositories: polyDB, Kreuzer–Skarke, and CYTools¶

    Toric geometry and mirror symmetry link smooth Fano and Calabi–Yau classifications to finite combinatorial lists of lattice polytopes:

    • polyDB (Andreas Paffenholz and the polymake team; arXiv:1711.02936) is a software-independent repository for discrete geometric objects with a web interface, REST API, and bindings for polymake, OSCAR, Python, and Julia. Its LatticePolytopes namespace includes the SmoothReflexive collection — cataloging smooth reflexive lattice polytopes (hence smooth toric Fano varieties) up to dimension 8 — as well as reflexive polytopes, combinatorial polytopes, matroids, and tropical objects.
    • Calabi–Yau data (Kreuzer–Skarke) (Maximilian Kreuzer and Harald Skarke; arXiv:hep-th/0002240, arXiv:math/0001106) hosts the complete classification of all 473,800,776 473,800,776 reflexive 4-polytopes and their associated Batyrev Calabi–Yau threefold hypersurfaces (yielding 30,108 30,108 distinct Hodge pairs (h1,1,h1,2) ( h 1 , 1 , h 1 , 2 ) ), together with reflexive 3-polytopes, reflexive Gorenstein cones, and the lattice-polytope package PALP.
    • CYTools (Demirtas–Rios-Tascon–McAllister; arXiv:2211.03823) provides a modern Python environment built to parse the Kreuzer–Skarke list at scale, compute fine regular star triangulations, construct the associated smooth toric ambient fans, and extract Mori/Kähler cones, intersection numbers, and Calabi–Yau invariants.

    Broader mathematical database models: LMFDB and MathBases¶

    Outside Fano geometry, the L-functions and Modular Forms Database (LMFDB) established the prevailing design template for collaborative mathematical databases: permanent object labels, explicit bidirectional links connecting distinct mathematical objects, contextual inline definitions, and machine-verifiable provenance. Across disciplines, MathBases maintains an open catalog indexing Fanography, hyperkaehler.info, GRDB, Kreuzer–Skarke, LMFDB, and related mathematical datasets by mathematical subject, maintainers, and access format.

    How GEOATOMS fits into the ecosystem¶

    Existing Fano and homogeneous-space databases focus primarily on classical geometry, birational classification, polytope combinatorics, derived-category decompositions, and one-parameter regularized quantum periods. GEOATOMS complements them by computing and archiving the multi-parameter small quantum cohomology operator on ambient cohomology, its spectral and quantum-connection invariants, and its coarse Hodge-atom decomposition.

    Navigating between identifiers across databases¶

    GEOATOMS cross-references three primary classification numbering schemes alongside geometric construction names. Each scheme carries its own conventions and scope caveats.

    CKP period buckets, GRDB 4D periods, and GEOATOMS fourfolds¶

    The prefix CKP refers to Coates–Kasprzyk–Prince, Four-dimensional Fano toric complete intersections (arXiv:1409.5030), which constructed smooth four-dimensional Fano complete intersections of codimension at most 4 in smooth toric Fano varieties and distinguished 738 regularized quantum period sequences (at least 527 yielding new Fano fourfolds). Their period sequences can be queried on GRDB's 4-dimensional quantum periods page and downloaded from doi:10.5281/zenodo.5708307.

    In GEOATOMS, Fourfolds includes 94 CKP-tagged toric complete-intersection presentations (stored in toric-fourfolds.json), such as the complete intersection of multidegrees (1,1),(1,1) ( 1 , 1 ) , ( 1 , 1 ) in P3×P3 P 3 × P 3 (CKP-189), with explicit fans, defining divisors, symbolic operators, and Hodge/atomic data.

    Caveat on period buckets. CKP numbers index period buckets, not proven isomorphism classes. Equality of regularized quantum periods G^X(t)=G^X′(t) G ^ X ( t ) = G ^ X ′ ( t ) is a necessary condition for deformation equivalence, not a sufficient one: distinct presentations or non-isomorphic varieties can share a period sequence. GEOATOMS therefore always identifies examples primarily by their explicit geometric construction and keeps distinct presentations separate, each retaining its own curve coordinates, Novikov divisors, and ordered cohomology basis (see sources and terminology).

    HLM fourfold cases and GEOATOMS fourfolds¶

    The prefix HLM refers to Hausen–Laface–Mauz, On smooth Fano fourfolds of Picard number two (arXiv:1907.08000), classifying smooth Fano fourfolds of Picard rank two with a general hypersurface Cox ring. GEOATOMS records explicit HLM constructions — including the Verra fourfold (HLM-29, a section of PP2(O⊕O(1)⊕3) P P 2 ( O ⊕ O ( 1 ) ⊕ 3 ) by 2ξ 2 ξ ) and HLM-27 — alongside their fans, Hodge diamonds, and exact quantum operators.

    Caveats on presentations and ambient sectors. Two technical conventions should be kept in mind when translating between external classifications and GEOATOMS:

    • Toric and projective-bundle conventions. Split projective bundles PB(E) P B ( E ) in GEOATOMS use the quotient convention, and identifying an ambient toric variety from a fan requires an invertible integral lattice change matching all rays and all maximal cones, not merely ray counts or primitive relations (geometric identification).
    • Ambient vs. full cohomology. Localization computes A(y) A ( y ) on the ambient image V=Hamb∗(X,Q) V = H amb ∗ ( X , Q ) and sums over target curve classes sharing the same ambient pushforward ι∗β′=β ι ∗ β ′ = β . When cross-checking GEOATOMS spectra against full Hodge diamonds or semiorthogonal decompositions on Fanography or Grassmannian.info, primitive cohomology and the genericity and cyclicity hypotheses stated in atomic invariants must be accounted for separately.

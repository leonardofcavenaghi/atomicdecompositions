    Localization on flag varieties¶

    Root systems describe the ambient flag variety, its Schubert classes and effective curves. Euler-twisted virtual localization supplies the invariants of a supported smooth complete intersection and its ambient quantum operator.

    The flag variety and its inputs¶

    Let F=G/P F = G / P , with T⊂B⊂P T ⊂ B ⊂ P , simple roots Π Π , and positive roots R+ R + . Set

    ΠF=Π∖ΠP,RF+=R+∖RP+,dim⁡F=|RF+|. Π F = Π ∖ Π P , R F + = R + ∖ R P + , dim ⁡ F = | R F + | .

    The minimal representatives WF W F of W/WP W / W P index the Schubert basis σw σ w , with codimσw=ℓ(w) codim σ w = ℓ ( w ) . The software's kept roots are ΠF Π F , entered as one-based ambient Dynkin-node numbers. Schubert basis indices are different from these node numbers.

    Curve coordinates use the reduced coroot lattice. A compact beta vector follows the declared kept-root order; the equivalent ambient vector has zeros on removed nodes. Line-bundle rows of K have one coefficient per kept root. Supported homogeneous bundle expressions use the fiber-weight and curve-restriction constructions described in the bundle API; fixed-point weight containment alone does not prove a geometric quotient-bundle construction.

    Novikov names and finite enumeration¶

    The theoretical variable is qβ q β . The software writes its monomials using the displayed y variables; these are Novikov names, distinct from additive coordinates for big quantum cohomology. In the default flag convention, type A retains kept-node labels and other families use consecutive kept-root names. Explicit ambient and compact conventions are documented in the notation guide.

    The dimension axiom bounds degrees contributing to multiplication by c1 c 1 by ∫βc^1(TX)≤dim⁡X+1 ∫ β c ^ 1 ( T X ) ≤ dim ⁡ X + 1 . A finite automatic search additionally requires positive bounds on the supported effective generators. Zero or negative directions cannot be cut off solely by that inequality. Preserve the actual curve list with the result.

    The recorded ambient Chern gcd is not automatically the intrinsic Fano index; for instance a conic has ambient gcd 1 in P2 P 2 , but intrinsic index 2. For a point this gcd does not define a geometric Fano index.

    Regularized-exponent multisets¶

    Write a supported formal factor as

    Mb,α≃Eλ(b)/u⊗Rb,λ. M b , α ≃ E λ ( b ) / u ⊗ R b , λ .

    Here λ(b) λ ( b ) is an eigenvalue of κb=Eu⋆b(−) κ b = Eu ⋆ b ( − ) ; on the small locus the Euler class is c1(TZ) c 1 ( T Z ) . If Rb,λ R b , λ is regular singular with rational residue eigenvalues, its regularized-exponent multiset is

    Fb,λ={{−θ+Z:θ is a residue eigenvalue of Rb,λ}}. F b , λ = { { − θ + Z : θ  is a residue eigenvalue of  R b , λ } } .

    Every occurrence is retained with its algebraic multiplicity. Thus {{0,12,12}} { { 0 , 1 2 , 1 2 } } has three entries; it must not be simplified to a two-element set. The software's admissible powers s=−θ s = − θ give these classes modulo Z Z . The powers and residue-class lists are multisets, not a termwise pairing of sorted lists.

    Catalogue summaries keep the specialization point and factor association. When one rational characteristic factor has several roots, the displayed multiset applies separately at each indicated eigenvalue. Partial and unresolved calculations remain labelled; a missing exponent is not zero.

    The unit cyclic determinant is

    Δ(b)=det(1,A(b)1,…,A(b)N−11), Δ ( b ) = det ( 1 , A ( b ) 1 , … , A ( b ) N − 1 1 ) ,

    where N N is the dimension of the space on which the matrix acts. Its nonvanishing proves that the unit is cyclic on that space. Identifying this with a generic Hodge factor additionally requires the ambient/Hodge comparison and persistence hypotheses. A numerical specialization used to display eigenvalues does not itself verify those hypotheses or the admissibility of a point in the theoretical parameter domain.

    Sources and verification¶

    References: B. Alexeev et al., On the atomic decomposition of complete intersection in flag varieties, Theorems 3.26 and 3.28 and Definitions 4.5–4.6. Virtual localization follows Graber–Pandharipande, Localization of virtual classes.

    The exact datasets provide coefficients, curve coordinates, ordered bases and grading. See software input/output formats and validation scope.

    Verification¶

    Retain the exact matrix together with its ordered basis, grading, curve list and Novikov-coordinate map. The automated checks compare exact localization results, validate catalogue formulas and exercise the displayed software inputs. A numerical specialization or a rendered spectrum does not supply the additional geometric hypotheses needed for a full atomic decomposition.

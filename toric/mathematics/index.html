    Localization on toric varieties¶

    A fan describes the ambient smooth projective toric variety and its divisor and curve lattices. A sum of globally generated line bundles defines the complete intersection whose genus-zero invariants are computed by Euler-twisted localization.

    Fan and divisor inputs¶

    Let Σ Σ be a complete smooth projective fan in a lattice N N , with primitive rays vρ v ρ , and let XΣ X Σ have dimension n n . The software preserves the ray order and enters each maximal cone by zero-based ray indices.

    Write Dρ D ρ for the invariant divisors. The rational Chow ring is

    A∗(XΣ)Q=Q[Dρ:ρ∈Σ(1)]/(IΣ+JΣ), A ∗ ( X Σ ) Q = Q [ D ρ : ρ ∈ Σ ( 1 ) ] / ( I Σ + J Σ ) ,

    where IΣ I Σ is the Stanley–Reisner ideal and JΣ J Σ is generated by ∑ρ⟨m,vρ⟩Dρ ∑ ρ ⟨ m , v ρ ⟩ D ρ , for m∈Hom(N,Z) m ∈ Hom ( N , Z ) .

    A full row of K describes a divisor Dℓ=∑ρkℓρDρ D ℓ = ∑ ρ k ℓ ρ D ρ , with one coefficient per ray. Short rows instead use the reported cohomology divisor basis. That basis must not be confused with the chosen Novikov divisors. Negative ray coefficients can represent a nef divisor: the relevant test is degree on invariant curves.

    Finite curve searches¶

    The dimension condition gives the bound (c1(TXΣ)−c1(E))⋅β≤dim⁡Y+1 ( c 1 ( T X Σ ) − c 1 ( E ) ) ⋅ β ≤ dim ⁡ Y + 1 for matrix contributions. If the residual divisor is ample on the ambient Mori cone, this yields a finite ambient search. If Y Y is Fano but the residual is not ample on XΣ X Σ , the additional positivity hypothesis of Proposition B.3 is relevant; target Fano positivity alone is not an ambient stopping rule.

    Keep the returned curve list and enumeration mode. Supported modes distinguish positive ambient bounds, conditional Mori searches, suggested inequalities and ample-degree cutoffs. A sampled inequality check is not a proof of the Mori cone; a cutoff is exact only on its selected region.

    Regularized-exponent multisets¶

    Write a supported formal factor as

    Mb,α≃Eλ(b)/u⊗Rb,λ. M b , α ≃ E λ ( b ) / u ⊗ R b , λ .

    Here λ(b) λ ( b ) is an eigenvalue of κb=Eu⋆b(−) κ b = Eu ⋆ b ( − ) ; on the small locus the Euler class is c1(TZ) c 1 ( T Z ) . If Rb,λ R b , λ is regular singular with rational residue eigenvalues, its regularized-exponent multiset is

    Fb,λ={{−θ+Z:θ is a residue eigenvalue of Rb,λ}}. F b , λ = { { − θ + Z : θ  is a residue eigenvalue of  R b , λ } } .

    Every occurrence is retained with its algebraic multiplicity. Thus {{0,12,12}} { { 0 , 1 2 , 1 2 } } has three entries; it must not be simplified to a two-element set. The software's admissible powers s=−θ s = − θ give these classes modulo Z Z . The powers and residue-class lists are multisets, not a termwise pairing of sorted lists.

    Catalogue summaries keep the specialization point and factor association. When one rational characteristic factor has several roots, the displayed multiset applies separately at each indicated eigenvalue. Partial and unresolved calculations remain labelled; a missing exponent is not zero.

    The unit cyclic determinant is

    Δ(b)=det(1,A(b)1,…,A(b)N−11), Δ ( b ) = det ( 1 , A ( b ) 1 , … , A ( b ) N − 1 1 ) ,

    where N N is the dimension of the space on which the matrix acts. Its nonvanishing proves that the unit is cyclic on that space. Identifying this with a generic Hodge factor additionally requires the ambient/Hodge comparison and persistence hypotheses. A numerical specialization used to display eigenvalues does not itself verify those hypotheses or the admissibility of a point in the theoretical parameter domain.

    Hodge data and the scope of rationality statements¶

    Fan cohomology gives the ambient Hodge diamond. Full complete-intersection Hodge calculations require the implemented positivity and dimension hypotheses; some nef fourfold records instead cite literature values. In fivefolds, primitive middle H5 H 5 is odd and lies outside the displayed ambient even-sector matrix.

    The cyclic-factor obstruction applies to dimensions 2–4 and requires a cyclic generic Hodge factor of unramified exponential type with rational residues. Its separate fivefold criterion, Theorem 4.10 attributed to Cavenaghi–Katzarkov–Kontsevich, assumes a coarse Hodge atom α α with ρα≤3 ρ α ≤ 3 and [t3]Pα(t)>0 [ t 3 ] P α ( t ) > 0 . A small-matrix eigenvalue or a computed exponent list alone does not establish these hypotheses. Apply this criterion only after identifying the full coarse atom and its Hodge polynomial.

    Sources and reproducibility¶

    References: M. Brockhof, G. Galindo, L. Katzarkov and P. A. Muniz Martins, On the atomic decomposition of complete intersections in toric varieties, Theorems 3.12 and 3.17, Definition 4.2, §4.3 and Appendix B. Virtual localization follows Liu, Liu–Sheshmani and Graber–Pandharipande. For homogeneous spaces, see B. Alexeev et al., On the atomic decomposition of complete intersection in flag varieties.

    The universal catalogue is organized by geometric dimension and construction. Its CKP and HLM identifiers refer to Coates–Kasprzyk–Prince, Four-dimensional Fano toric complete intersections, and Hausen–Laface–Mauz, On smooth Fano fourfolds of Picard number two. CKP period buckets do not identify varieties solely by equality of periods.

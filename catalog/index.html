    Reading the computations¶

    For a variety X X of dimension d d , let (e1,…,eN) ( e 1 , … , e N ) be the returned homogeneous basis of its ambient cohomology. The matrix

    A(y)=[c1(TX)⋆(−)]amb,G=diag(codim(ei)−d2) A ( y ) = [ c 1 ( T X ) ⋆ ( − ) ] amb , G = diag ( codim ( e i ) − d 2 )

    represents the ambient projection of small quantum multiplication by the first Chern class, and G G is its grading. Column j j of A A is the image of ej e j ; row i i gives the coefficient of ei e i . The parameters y y are the Novikov coordinates; exact software outputs may name them y1, y2, and so on.

    At a stated coordinate assignment a a , write A(a) A ( a ) for the specialized matrix. Its characteristic polynomial is χA(a)(z)=det(zIN−A(a))=∏jpj(z)mj χ A ( a ) ( z ) = det ( z I N − A ( a ) ) = ∏ j p j ( z ) m j , where IN I N is the identity matrix and the pj p j are monic, pairwise coprime, square-free factors. Each root λ λ of pj p j is an eigenvalue of multiplicity mj m j . If 1 1 is the coordinate column of the unit, the unit cyclic determinant is

    ΔA(a)=det[1,A(a)1,…,A(a)N−11]. Δ A ( a ) = det [ 1 , A ( a ) 1 , … , A ( a ) N − 1 1 ] .

    Its nonvanishing proves that these quantum powers span the computed space. The regularized-exponent multiset Fa,λ F a , λ records the classes −θ+Z − θ + Z of residue eigenvalues θ θ of the regular-singular connection factor at λ λ , with repetitions retained. For a coarse Hodge atom α α , ρα ρ α counts rational Hodge-invariant directions, and Pα(t)=∑p,qhp,q(Eα)tp−q P α ( t ) = ∑ p , q h p , q ( E α ) t p − q records the Hodge-cocharacter weights with positive multiplicities.

    The matrix size N N is the ambient cohomology dimension, which may differ from both the full rational Hodge-invariant dimension and the total cohomology dimension. Atomic interpretations drawn from these matrices require the cohomological and genericity hypotheses stated with each result. A zero cyclic determinant at a particular point does not imply vanishing at every point.

    The Recorded Chern index uses the presentation's convention. In the original flag collection it is an ambient Chern gcd: a conic has ambient gcd one in P2 P 2 , but intrinsic Fano index two. A point has no geometric Fano index defined by that gcd. In the table, h(1,1) h ( 1 , 1 ) means h1,1(X) h 1 , 1 ( X ) ; a dash indicates that no value is asserted in this summary.
